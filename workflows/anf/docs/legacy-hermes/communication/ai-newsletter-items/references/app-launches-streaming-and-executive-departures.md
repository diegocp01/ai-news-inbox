# App Launches, Synthetic Streaming, and Executive Departures

Use these checks for fast-moving launch and personnel claims that originate on social media.

## App-based agent launches

- Prefer a live Apple App Store, Microsoft Store, or equivalent first-party marketplace listing when the announcement URL is policy-blocked.
- Verify the exact app name, developer/seller, supported devices, availability state, pricing or in-app purchases, and the capabilities explicitly described in the listing.
- Do not infer plan eligibility from the marketplace when it is absent; verify it from the announcement or credible same-day coverage and attribute it.
- Marketplace screenshots are strong story-specific images. Read the rendered page’s image URLs, choose a screenshot illustrating the reported capability, request a larger `mzstatic` size variant when available, and navigate to the direct asset to confirm that it resolves and has useful dimensions.
- Separate platform availability precisely: iPhone, macOS, Windows, Linux, and Android may have different launch states. “Desktop” does not automatically mean every desktop OS.

## AI-generated streaming channels

- Distinguish the synthetic programming from the advertising inventory. A FAST channel can carry AI-generated shows while still inserting conventionally produced ads.
- Verify “AI-generated commercials” by inspecting the stream or credible first-hand coverage; do not infer it merely because the channel’s shows are synthetic.
- Report whether viewers can select titles or only watch a linear feed, who curates or supplies the programs, and whether creators are paid.
- If a viral claim overstates what is synthetic, correct it neutrally in the summary and identify what direct observation established.

## Public AI build livestreams

- Open the official event or registration page and verify the dates, daily hours, named participants, stated deliverable, schedule, and whether attendance is online, in person, or both; social summaries often compress a multi-track event into one continuous demonstration.
- Distinguish humans using an agent from an agent autonomously creating a company. Say that the named team will “attempt” or “set out” to build when the event has not happened, and identify the AI’s supporting role rather than implying independent execution.
- Separate “build a company” framing from the concrete work promised—such as a business plan, product decisions, coding, deployment, sales, support, or marketing. Do not treat a product prototype or showcase as proof that an operating company was created.
- Prefer a compliant official event or registration permalink over the vendor’s blocked `.ai` page or a social post when it documents the exact current event. Use its verified event artwork when it resolves directly and is story-specific.

## Executive departures and title drift

- Establish the person’s title on the announcement date, not only the best-known historical title.
- Check for recent reorganizations: a “COO resigns” claim may actually concern a former COO who had already moved to special projects while another executive assumed the operating duties.
- Prefer wording such as “former COO” or “longtime executive” when the operational role had already changed.
- Verify tenure, stated reason, effective timing, successor or redistribution of responsibilities, and whether the person is fully leaving, taking leave, becoming an adviser, or only stepping down from one role.
- Avoid turning “to start something new” into a confirmed new company or venture unless the person or reporting explicitly says so.
