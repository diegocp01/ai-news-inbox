# ChatGPT Sites analytics and AI-cheating claims

## ChatGPT Sites analytics

- Do not confuse **Sites analytics** with ChatGPT Enterprise/Edu **workspace analytics**.
- The durable, compliant source is `https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/sites`, specifically **Review Site analytics**.
- Sites records traffic automatically without an analytics SDK.
- Reported metrics: total unique visitors, page views, both over time, adjustable date range and granularity.
- Access: a Site’s **More actions > Analytics** in ChatGPT web or desktop. No standalone analytics view in the CLI or IDE extension.
- Suitable OG image: `https://learn.chatgpt.com/og/docs/sites.png`.
- If the user repeats the same intake line, treat it as one item rather than producing duplicates.

## Viral professor / hidden-prompt stories

For claims that a professor caught students using AI through hidden white text:

1. Separate **failing a portion** of an exam from failing the whole exam or course.
2. Verify whether the count spans one class or multiple classes.
3. Attribute the account to the professor when evidence is primarily a viral video or interview.
4. A copied hidden instruction strongly indicates prompt copying but is not a universal AI detector; avoid broad claims that all AI use was conclusively proven.
5. Record appeal outcomes when reported because they qualify the headline count.
6. Prefer a neutral title such as “Hidden Prompt Flags Suspected AI Use” over celebratory or accusatory language.

Session example: Alcorn State history professor Jason Gibson said 32 of 35 students across two classes lost credit on part of a midterm after answers included a hidden instruction to insert “Madagascar”; one grade was overturned on appeal.
